# Adviser research observations and lessons — batch 05

The largest unresolved question is **which adviser will personally deliver founder liquidity at commercially sensible fees**, not which adviser publishes the most transactions.

## Recommendations and lessons

Keep the first message strategic but specific: digital-trust software, a strategic shareholder and founder liquidity. The purpose is a fit conversation, not an early valuation negotiation or a roadmap pitch.

Use flexible USD 10–15M / approximately 25% only when helpful to qualify adviser economics. In a simplified all-secondary transaction with identical economic rights, that combination could imply USD 40–60M equity value; it is not a valuation conclusion. A mixed primary/secondary structure changes the cash and dilution calculation. Define the numbers before comparing proposals.

Separate adviser access from buyer fit. A strong M&A team may reach large software acquirers, while a local relationship adviser may reach UAE strategic groups; neither alone necessarily supplies both. Test a joint team rather than presuming a network will work as one organisation.

Require evidence at the level that matters: current executing people, actual adviser role, named closed case, shareholder cash and comparable economics. “We know the buyer” needs a useful transaction relationship, not just a LinkedIn connection.

Do not multiply investor outreach through several advisers before agreeing buyer ownership and exclusions. Earlier batch contacts can create duplicate approaches, fee claims or conflicting narratives.

Record what changes after conversations: willingness to take the mandate, practical minimum, software understanding, direct GCC relationships, actual senior involvement and fee basis. Move tiers when new evidence warrants it; preserve original source dates and reasons.
