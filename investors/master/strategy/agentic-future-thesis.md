# Circularo — Agentic Future Thesis

**Status:** Internal / Confidential  
**Purpose:** Explain Circularo's credible strategic expansion from digital signatures and trusted document workflows toward trusted execution for AI agents.  
**Last updated:** October 2026

---

## 1. Core Thesis

As AI evolves from generating information to taking actions on behalf of organizations, institutions will require infrastructure that can establish:

- identity;
- organizational context;
- authority;
- policy;
- controlled execution;
- evidence;
- accountability;
- durable trusted records.

These requirements are adjacent to trust foundations Circularo already provides.

Circularo's long-term opportunity is therefore not simply to add AI features to an eSignature product.

It is to become part of the **trust and execution infrastructure required when humans, enterprise systems and autonomous agents act on behalf of institutions.**

---

## 2. This Is Not an AI Pivot

The strategic evolution is:

**Digital Signatures  
→ Digital Trust  
→ Trusted Records / Institutional Evidence  
→ Trusted Execution  
→ Agentic Trusted Execution**

The existing business remains the foundation.

Circularo should never be positioned as abandoning eSignatures in order to become an AI startup.

Instead:

> Digital signatures forced Circularo to solve identity, authority, workflow, evidence and accountability for human transactions. The Agentic AI era requires many of the same trust primitives for machine-executed transactions.

## 4. The Emerging Institutional Problem

AI systems are moving through three stages:

**Assist → Decide → Act**

The risk changes materially when AI begins to **act**.

When an AI agent executes an institutional action, the organization needs to know:

- What agent is acting?
- Who does it represent?
- What organization or department does it belong to?
- What authority has been delegated?
- Under which policy?
- What limits apply?
- What information justified the action?
- Was additional approval required?
- What exactly was executed?
- What was the result?
- What evidence was created?
- Can the action be independently audited later?

This is the **Trusted Execution problem**.

## 5. Circularo's Emerging Trust Stack

### HOROS — Organizational Context

Conceptually answers:

> **Who sits where?**

HOROS provides organizational context for humans, systems and agents.

Potential concepts include:

- organizations;
- subsidiaries;
- departments;
- teams;
- roles;
- organizational relationships;
- structural scope.

---

### AUTHOS — Authority

Conceptually answers:

> **Who or what has authority to do what?**

Potential concepts include:

- signing authority;
- approval authority;
- delegation;
- monetary limits;
- transaction classes;
- document classes;
- policy constraints;
- temporary authority;
- agent permissions.

---

### ACTUS — Trusted Execution Protocol

Conceptually answers:

> **How does an authorized action happen?**

ACTUS defines how actions are:

- requested;
- authorized;
- policy-checked;
- approved where necessary;
- executed;
- acknowledged;
- failed/retried;
- evidenced.

ACTUS should remain conceptually separate from eDoc.

---

### eDoc — Canonical Trust Object

Conceptually answers:

> **What is true about this transaction?**

eDoc represents the transaction/document itself:

- intent;
- participants;
- authority;
- state;
- content;
- evidence;
- signatures;
- timestamps;
- visibility;
- lifecycle.

---

### SANTOS — Sentient Interaction & Orchestration

SANTOS represents the intelligent interaction/orchestration layer.

Conceptually it can:

- understand human intent;
- assemble context;
- interact with enterprise systems;
- coordinate tools and agents;
- initiate governed actions;
- present outcomes to humans.

---

### Trusted Records

Conceptually answers:

> **What can the institution prove happened?**

Trusted Records preserve durable institutional evidence of actions and outcomes.

## 7. Why eDoc Matters

Traditional documents primarily represent content.

eDoc is intended to represent the **transaction itself**.

It should bind:

- what was intended;
- by whom;
- under what authority;
- what information was involved;
- how the transaction evolved;
- what decisions were made;
- what was executed;
- what evidence resulted.

The canonical state should be both:

- machine-readable;
- human-understandable.

PDFs and other human-readable artifacts can become first-class projections of this state rather than necessarily being the source of truth.

This becomes increasingly important when machines participate directly in transactions.

---

## 8. Why ACTUS Should Remain Separate from eDoc

The conceptual distinction is:

> **eDoc defines what is true.**

> **ACTUS defines how trusted action happens.**

Keeping these layers separate enables:

- human actors;
- system actors;
- AI agents;
- different execution mechanisms;
- delegated authority;
- policy checks;
- approval gates;
- execution acknowledgements;
- failures;
- evidence generation.

eDoc can natively use ACTUS without absorbing the execution protocol itself.

---

## 9. Trusted Records as Institutional Memory

The rise of autonomous systems makes trusted records more important, not less.

When actions become faster and increasingly machine-driven, institutions need durable evidence of:

- instruction;
- context;
- authority;
- policy;
- decision;
- execution;
- result;
- exception;
- human intervention.

Circularo already has experience preserving evidence around signed transactions.

Trusted Records generalize this principle beyond signatures.

## 10. Why Circularo Has a Credible Right to Explore This

The thesis is **not**:

> Circularo does eSignatures, therefore Circularo will win Agentic AI.

The actual reasoning is:

1. Circularo already operates where actions have legal and institutional consequences.
2. Circularo already works with identity, authority, approvals and evidence.
3. Circularo supports human and API/system transactions.
4. Circularo operates in sovereign and regulated environments.
5. eDoc has already been conceived as a structured, verifiable and AI-readable transaction model.
6. Agentic execution requires similar trust primitives at a broader abstraction level.

Therefore the adjacency is credible enough to pursue and validate.

## 14. Validation Milestones

The thesis becomes more valuable as Circularo demonstrates successive milestones:

1. **eDoc canonical transaction model operational**  
   eDoc moves from architecture/specification into production use for real transactions.

2. **Explicit authority model operational**  
   Circularo can represent who or what is authorized to perform particular actions, under defined conditions and limits.

3. **Organizational context operational**  
   HOROS or equivalent capabilities provide machine-readable organizational context connecting entities, departments, roles, humans, systems and agents.

4. **Trusted Records architecture operational**  
   Circularo can preserve authoritative institutional evidence beyond traditional signed-document records.

5. **AI actions governed through authority and policy controls**  
   AI-assisted actions cannot simply execute because an LLM requested them. Authority, policy and permissions are checked before execution.

6. **ACTUS implemented for real workflows**  
   Trusted execution becomes an explicit protocol/model rather than implicit application logic.

7. **External systems and agents invoke Trusted Execution**  
   Third-party AI agents and enterprise systems can request actions through Circularo while Circularo governs authorization, execution and evidence.

8. **Sovereign or regulated enterprise design partner**  
   At least one strategically important customer validates the architecture against a real institutional use case.

9. **Cross-organizational Trusted Execution**  
   Circularo can govern actions involving multiple organizations with different authority structures, policies and evidence requirements.

10. **Repeatable commercial revenue**  
    Trusted Execution becomes a commercially purchased Circularo capability rather than solely an R&D initiative.

These milestones should eventually become part of the investor roadmap.

## 17. Relationship to MCP and Agent Tooling

Protocols such as MCP make it increasingly easy for AI agents to discover and invoke tools.

That solves an important interoperability problem.

It does not by itself answer institutional questions such as:

- Is this agent authorized?
- On whose behalf is it acting?
- Does that person have authority?
- Is the requested action within policy?
- Is additional approval required?
- What evidence must be preserved?
- What happened after execution?

Circularo's opportunity is therefore potentially complementary to agent/tool protocols.

Conceptually:

```text id="kjdfha"
AI Agent
   ↓
Agent / Tool Protocol
   ↓
Circularo Trusted Execution
   ↓
Authority + Policy + Organizational Context
   ↓
Enterprise / Government System
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Trusted Record
```

Circularo should not compete unnecessarily with emerging agent protocols.

It should provide the trust layer those protocols may need for institutional use.

Governments increasingly want the productivity benefits of AI while maintaining:

- sovereignty;
- control;
- accountability;
- security;
- regulatory compliance;
- institutional authority.

The problem becomes significantly harder when AI systems move from analyzing government information to **taking government actions**.

Circularo's sovereign deployment experience could create an important strategic position here.

This is particularly aligned with Circularo's existing multi-tenant government architecture.

It remains a long-term thesis requiring customer validation.

## 21. Key Risks

### Technology Risk

The proposed architecture may prove more difficult to implement than expected.

### Category Risk

Customers may solve agent governance through existing IAM, security, workflow or AI platforms.

### Timing Risk

The market may take longer to require formal Trusted Execution infrastructure.

### Standards Risk

Other protocols or standards may become dominant.

### Competitive Risk

Large cloud, identity, cybersecurity or AI-platform companies could move into the category.

### Product Focus Risk

Pursuing the future architecture could distract Circularo from monetizing its existing sovereign and SaaS opportunities.

### Narrative Risk

Overstating the Agentic AI opportunity could reduce investor credibility.
