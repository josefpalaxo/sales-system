# Circularo Narrative Usage Guidance

## 1. Use the hierarchy consistently

Do not use every strategic label as a synonym for Circularo.

| Level | Preferred term | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market context | **Digital trust infrastructure** | Names the broad space. |
| Strategic requirement | **Trusted execution infrastructure** | Names what automated and agentic work increasingly requires. |
| Platform proposition | **Circularo Unified Trust Platform** | Names the connected platform proposition; owner validation required. |
| Architectural role | **Trusted Execution Layer** | Explains where Circularo connects actors, controls, action, and evidence. |
| Strategic outcome | **System of Authority** | Describes the institutional result; not yet an approved category claim. |

Use only the level needed for the audience. A SaaS buyer may need “managed trusted execution,” not the full category hierarchy.

## 2. Trusted action versus trusted execution

### Preferred

- **Trusted action** — the specific act.
- **Trusted business action** — the specific customer-valued act when technical events also appear in the conversation.
- **Trusted execution** — the governed lifecycle around that action.
- **Trusted outcome** — the resulting business or institutional state reached through the required controls.
- **Verifiable outcome** — an outcome supported by connected evidence and a trusted record.
- **Trust event** — one underlying identity, approval, signature, seal, timestamp, verification, evidence, or preservation operation.

### House rule

Replace **consequential action** and **consequential business action** with **trusted action** and **trusted business action** in new audience-facing material.

Do not rewrite historical source material merely to enforce the house rule. Apply it when content is consolidated, revised, or published.

### Example

> Start with one trusted action. Define the authority and assurance it requires. Design the trusted execution around it. Identify the trust events and evidence needed to prove the outcome.

The preferred value chain is:

> Trusted action → Trusted execution → Trusted and verifiable outcome → Trusted record

Do not use **trusted outcome** to imply that an outcome is correct, lawful, effective, or independently verified unless the required evidence supports that claim. “Trusted” describes the governed path; “verifiable” describes the ability to substantiate the result later.

## 3. Identity is not authority

Use the sequence:

| Question | Term |
| --- | --- |
| Who or what are you? | Identity |
| Whom do you represent? | Representation and role |
| What may you do? | Delegated authority |
| Under which conditions? | Policy |
| Which decision or gate is required? | Approval and assurance |
| What happened and what remains provable? | Execution, evidence, and audit |

Avoid language implying that authentication or identification alone authorizes an action.

## 4. Position signatures correctly

### Preferred

> The signature is one important trust event within a governed lifecycle.

> Trust starts before the signature and continues through evidence, preservation, and later verification.

### Avoid

- “Signatures are no longer important.”
- “Circularo replaces eSignature.”
- Any wording that dismisses the category through which many buyers first understand Circularo.

Broaden the frame without devaluing signatures.

## 5. Keep the AI boundary precise

### Preferred

> Circularo governs and evidences what an agent is permitted to execute.

> The model may reason; the institution remains in control of what executes.

### Avoid

- “Circularo makes AI trustworthy.”
- “Circularo governs AI reasoning.”
- “Circularo guarantees AI accuracy or safety.”
- “Agentic” as a synonym for unrestricted autonomous action.

When using **KNOW → ADVISE → ACT**, state that ACT is bounded by identity, delegated authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit. Present current, integration-dependent, in-development, and directional capabilities separately.

## 6. Use trust as a concrete mechanism

Avoid unsupported or empty phrases such as:

- trusted by design;
- complete trust;
- absolute trust;
- trust everywhere;
- fully trusted AI;
- seamless sovereign intelligent trust orchestration.

Replace them with the concrete dimension:

- actor identity;
- represented authority;
- transaction policy;
- approval or assurance;
- execution control;
- retained evidence;
- auditability;
- verification;
- control boundary.

## 7. Keep delivery narratives distinct

### Circularo SaaS

Lead with fragmented digital work, a managed trust environment, continuous evidence, adoption, and a first trusted workflow.

Do not lead with sovereignty or imply customer operation of the infrastructure.

### Circularo Sovereign

Lead with a defined control boundary across infrastructure, data, identity, keys, policy, integrations, evidence, AI, operations, and jurisdiction.

Do not reduce sovereignty to data residency or a generic on-premises label.

### Sovereign Trust Shared Services

Lead with reusable capabilities, multi-entity governance, entity-owned services, common evidence principles, and progressive adoption.

Do not describe it as simply “Circularo Sovereign for more tenants.” Do not imply unrestricted cross-entity data sharing.

## 8. API language

### Narrative use

Use **programmable trust** to explain how the same trust rules and evidence model can serve people, portals, applications, enterprise systems, and approved AI agents.

### Commercial use

The API is a channel, not a Product, Plan, or subscription model. Access and consumption are governed separately. For exact commercial treatment, use:

- `commercial-rule:api-access`
- `commercial-rule:automated-transactions`
- `commercial-rule:add-on-plan-eligibility`

Do not infer that narrative phrases such as “one API” include access, volume, automation, or trust-service consumption.

## 9. Commercial terminology boundaries

Use the ordered commercial hierarchy exactly:

> Solution → Product → Edition → Plan → Subscription → Subscription Item

Do not use these terms interchangeably.

- A **Solution** frames the customer problem and value.
- A **Product** is the market-facing offering.
- An **Edition** defines functional possibility.
- A **Plan** packages and commercializes an Edition.
- A **Subscription** binds the contractual arrangement.
- A **Subscription Item** is billed or metered.

Do not copy prices, limits, eligibility, plan mappings, entitlement rules, or policy details into narrative content. Reference the stable commercial record.

## 10. Capitalization

Capitalize only deliberate names and defined models:

- Circularo

- Trusted Execution Layer

- Regular User, Lite User, External User / Recipient when referring to governed commercial user types

Use sentence case for generic concepts:

- trusted execution
- trusted action

- trust event
- institutional memory

- continuous evidence chain
- sovereignty boundary

## 11. Preferred American English

Use these forms unless a target market requires localization:

- organization
- authorized
- authorization
- analyze
- centralized
- license / licensed

Retain official external names and legal terminology in their source spelling.

## 12. Punctuation and cadence

- Use arrows for a real progression or causal sequence, not decoration.
- Use centered dots for a compact set of peer concepts: **Identity · Authority · Policy · Approval · Evidence**.
- Use a maximum of one major coined concept per headline or paragraph.
- Prefer complete plain-language sentences around teasers.
- Avoid repeated “From X to Y” constructions on adjacent slides or sections.
- Avoid dense noun stacks such as “sovereign intelligent trust orchestration platform.”

## 13. Claim-state language

| Evidence state | Preferred language |
| --- | --- |
| Approved current capability | “Circularo supports…” followed by the governed scope. |
| Configurable or integration-dependent | “Circularo can be configured to…” or “The solution can connect to… subject to validation.” |
| Directional | “Circularo is designed to…” or “The platform can provide a foundation for…” |
| Conceptual model | “The narrative proposes…” or “A trusted-execution model can…” |
| Unknown | State that validation or evidence is required. |

Do not make repetition substitute for evidence.

## 14. Disallowed shortcuts

Avoid introducing competing category labels without an explicit messaging decision:

- trust operating system;
- execution fabric;
- autonomy control plane;
- AI trust engine;
- sovereign trust mesh;
- intelligent trust orchestration layer.

Avoid external superlatives unless governed evidence exists:

- first;
- only;
- leading;
- largest;
- national scale;
- government proven;
- enterprise proven;
- compliant by default;
- guaranteed;
- fully sovereign.

## 15. Discovery prompts by topic

### Trusted action

- What is the action the institution needs to complete and prove?
- Who or what may prepare, initiate, approve, and execute it?
- On whose authority does each actor proceed?
- Which policy, approval, and assurance apply?
- What must remain provable afterwards?

### SaaS

- Where do versions, approvals, signatures, and evidence become disconnected today?
- Which first trusted workflow would show measurable value?
- Which security, data-location, identity, integration, migration, and operating boundaries must be validated?

### Sovereign

- Which controls cannot be delegated?
- Where must infrastructure, data, identity, keys, policy, evidence, AI, and operations remain?
- Which existing national or enterprise trust infrastructure should be preserved and connected?
- Who owns deployment, upgrades, continuity, key management, and support?

### Shared services

- Which trust capabilities are being rebuilt independently by each entity?
- Which capabilities should be common, and which policies and records remain entity-owned?
- How will entities be onboarded, funded, supported, and governed?
- Which initial services can prove reuse without forcing immediate ecosystem-wide migration?

### Agentic systems

- What may the agent know, advise, prepare, initiate, approve, or execute?
- Which person or institution delegates that mandate?
- Where are the required human gates?
- Which evidence must connect the agent, authority, input context, policy, and result?

## 16. Content review checklist

Before using new Circularo narrative content, confirm:

- The intended audience and buying problem are clear.
- “Trusted action” is used instead of “consequential action.”
- Identity and authority are distinct.
- Trust is explained through concrete controls and evidence.
- Signatures are broadened into the lifecycle without being dismissed.
- AI claims stop at permitted execution and retained evidence.
- The correct delivery narrative is used.
- APIs are not confused with commercial entitlements.
- Strategic terms are not presented as approved product categories without validation.
- Current capability, integration dependency, and direction are visibly separated.
- Commercial rules point to stable IDs rather than being copied.
- A concrete next step closes the narrative.
