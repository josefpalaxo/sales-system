# Narrative Dictionary Observations and Recommendations

## Observations

### 1. The core narrative is coherent

The strongest material now forms one consistent logic:

> Autonomy → Authority gap → Trusted execution → Evidence → Trusted records → Institutional memory → Trusted AI context → Governed action

This is a stronger common spine than the earlier practice of developing each deck as an independent narrative.

### 2. The latest language decision improves clarity

Replacing **consequential action** with **trusted action** makes the copy more natural, positive, and reusable across audiences. It also creates a useful distinction:

- trusted action — the act;
- trusted execution — the governed lifecycle;
- trusted outcome — the resulting business or institutional state;
- verifiable outcome — the result supported by connected evidence and a trusted record;
- trust event — the underlying operation.

This adds the missing customer-value layer between execution and the durable record.

The source files still contain many historical uses of “consequential.” They should remain intact for provenance and be updated only when those working artifacts are deliberately revised.

### 3. Circularo has a useful five-level positioning hierarchy

The current body of work supports a disciplined hierarchy from market context to strategic outcome. The risk is not a shortage of strong terms; it is allowing every term to become a headline or product name.

The most important terminology approval remains:

1. Digital trust infrastructure.
2. Trusted execution infrastructure.
3. Circularo Unified Trust Platform.
4. Trusted Execution Layer.
5. System of Authority.

### 4. “System of Authority” is powerful but still gated

It is the most distinctive strategic outcome because it answers institutional questions that systems of record do not. It should remain an outcome until product, sales, legal, and messaging owners validate external category use.

### 5. The actor-authority distinction is the most defensible AI message

“Capability is not authority” and “Intelligence does not create institutional authority” give Circularo a credible role without claiming control over model reasoning, truthfulness, or safety.

### 6. Evidence is the connective tissue

Evidence connects every major narrative:

- SaaS: fragmented work becomes a continuous evidence chain.
- Sovereign: execution evidence remains inside the control boundary.
- Shared Services: common evidence principles strengthen multi-entity accountability.
- Agentic: actions remain attributable and provable.
- Commercial model: the customer values the trusted and verifiable outcome; trusted execution is the governed mechanism, and trust events are the underlying operations.

### 7. The three delivery narratives are genuinely different

They should not be reduced to hosting options:

- SaaS answers how to simplify trusted work through managed delivery.
- Sovereign answers how to retain a required control boundary.
- Shared Services answers how to establish reusable capability and governance across entities.

### 8. Commercial and narrative vocabularies touch but must not merge

“Trusted execution,” “trust event,” and “programmable trust” can help explain value, but they must not silently redefine governed Plans, subscription models, Add-Ons, API entitlements, or consumption rules.

### 9. The governed repository still lacks approved external narrative truth

The company record is reviewed and explicitly incomplete. The major narrative and slide assets are working or draft material. The catalog is therefore a coordination tool, not an external publication authority.

## Recommendations

### 1. Approve a controlled vocabulary record

Promote only the smallest stable terminology set after owner review. The initial governed record should define:

- trusted action;
- trusted execution;
- trust event;
- trusted outcome;
- verifiable outcome;
- Trusted Execution Layer;
- continuous trust and evidence chain;
- trusted record;
- institutional memory;
- governed action;
- programmable trust; and
- System of Authority as a gated strategic outcome.

Do not promote the entire phrase library as one canonical record.

### 2. Approve the positioning hierarchy separately

Create a messaging decision for the five-level hierarchy. This decision should state which terms are:

- public market language;
- approved positioning;
- internal architecture language;
- strategic direction; or
- prohibited until validation.

### 3. Create a narrative lint checklist

Turn the rules in `usage-guidance.md` into a lightweight review checklist for decks, web copy, brochures, proposals, and partner material. At minimum, flag:

- consequential action;
- identity used as authority;
- claims that Circularo governs AI reasoning;
- sovereignty equated only with data residency;
- API access confused with included consumption;
- System of Authority used as an approved category;
- unsupported superlatives and proof claims.

### 4. Add approved product-capability mappings

For each core narrative concept, identify whether the supporting capability is:

- available now;
- configurable;
- integration-dependent;
- in development;
- directional; or
- unsupported.

This mapping is the main prerequisite for turning the vocabulary into externally reusable copy.

### 5. Build an approved proof vocabulary

The current catalog is strong on concepts and weak on governed proof. A separate proof catalog should define which customer, deployment, security, compliance, scale, integration, and outcome statements may be used, by audience and market.

### 6. Test the terms with real audiences

Validate at least these distinctions with sellers, partners, customers, and prospects:

- trusted action versus trusted execution;
- Trusted Execution Layer versus Unified Trust Platform;
- System of Authority as an outcome;
- managed trusted execution;
- customer-controlled trusted execution;
- trust as a shared service.

Capture which term the audience understands without explanation and which requires a concrete example.

### 7. Define the relationship between “trusted record” and product record types

The narrative term is currently broader than any governed product schema. Product owners should specify whether “trusted record” is:

- a conceptual outcome;
- a formal record type;
- a bundle of document, evidence, audit, and metadata objects; or
- different things in different platform contexts.

Until then, use it as a narrative outcome, not a data-model promise.

### 8. Validate the assurance and execution-profile model

The model is valuable for partner discovery and commercial explanation, but it needs product, legal, jurisdictional, and provider validation before external standardization.

### 9. Keep one source of working vocabulary

Use this folder as the working catalog and avoid creating competing glossaries inside individual deck folders. Deck references should link back here once the team begins maintaining the catalog operationally.

### 10. Review cadence

Review the working catalog whenever one of these changes:

- positioning approval;
- product-capability map;
- commercial model;
- delivery-model definition;
- AI capability state;
- evidence or proof pack;
- a terminology decision from the messaging owner.

When the core terminology stabilizes, extract approved entries into the smallest governed shared-messaging record and leave exploratory phrases under `work/`.
