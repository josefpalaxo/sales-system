# Circularo Vocabulary

## How to read this catalog

Each entry answers four questions: what the term means, how Circularo uses it, where it belongs, and how mature the language is.

The terms are grouped by narrative topic rather than by repository folder. They are not interchangeable slogans; together they form a hierarchy.

## 2. The trusted-execution model

| Term | Working definition | Use | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trusted action** | A specific business act that must be attributable, authorized, governed, executed, and evidenced. | Preferred audience-facing term. Examples can include an approval, agreement execution, organizational seal, regulated submission, or agent-initiated workflow. | House recommendation. |
| **Trusted business action** | A more explicit form of “trusted action” used when the business outcome must be distinguished from technical operations. | Use in commercial, discovery, and partner contexts. | House recommendation. |
| **Business action** | The institutional act the customer needs to complete. | Use when trust is discussed separately; otherwise prefer “trusted business action.” | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted execution** | The governed lifecycle that connects authority and policy to actual execution, evidence, and later verification. | Use for the end-to-end execution mechanism, not for a single signature API call or the resulting business state. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted Execution** | The same concept used as a named narrative model. | Capitalize only when deliberately naming the model; use sentence case generically. | House recommendation. |
| **Trust event** | An underlying trust operation within a trusted execution, such as identity verification, approval, signing, sealing, timestamping, evidence generation, or preservation. | Distinguish the technical operation from the customer-valued outcome. | Working narrative; commercial-model validation required before pricing use. |
| **Outcome** | The business or institutional state produced when an action completes, such as an agreement executed, approval granted, official record issued, or service completed. | Name the business result without implying that its trust or evidence has been established. | House recommendation. |
| **Trusted outcome** | An outcome produced through an execution governed by the required identity, authority, policy, approval, assurance, and evidence controls. | Use when emphasizing confidence in how the result was reached. | House recommendation. |
| **Verifiable outcome** | An outcome that can be substantiated later through connected execution evidence and a trusted record. | Use when later proof, audit, or independent checking is central. Avoid implying legal validity or third-party verification unless supported. | House recommendation. |
| **Trusted and verifiable outcome** | The combined customer-value expression: a business result reached under the required controls and supported by evidence that can be checked later. | Preferred when summarizing the value created by trusted execution. | House recommendation; capability and evidence claims require validation. |
| **Execution lifecycle** | The complete path from preparation and authority through review, approval, execution, evidence, preservation, retrieval, and later use. | Use when the exact visible steps vary by audience. | Working narrative. |
| **Trust chain** | The connected sequence of controls and events that makes an action attributable and provable. | Prefer a concrete chain immediately after the term. | Working narrative. |
| **Continuous trust and evidence chain** | The uninterrupted connection between process context, decisions, identities, permissions, trust events, records, and evidence. | Preferred full form when explaining the mechanism. | House recommendation. |
| **Continuous evidence chain** | Shorter form emphasizing what remains connected and provable across the lifecycle. | Use after the full concept has been established. | Working narrative. |
| **Governed lifecycle** | An execution lifecycle bounded by authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit. | Useful when describing why the signature is only one event. | Working narrative. |
| **Full execution** | The complete business process and evidence context, not only the final signature or file. | Use in contrasts with isolated eSignature. | Working narrative. |
| **Execution context** | The actors, roles, authority, versions, decisions, approvals, trust events, evidence, and history surrounding an executed action. | Use when explaining the difference between a file and a trusted record. | House recommendation. |

## 3. Actors, authority, and control

| Term | Working definition | Use | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Actor** | A person, organization, application, or AI agent that prepares, initiates, approves, executes, or receives an action. | Use as the neutral umbrella term. | Working narrative. |
| **Identity** | Establishes who or what the actor is. | Never imply that identity alone grants permission to act. | Working narrative. |
| **Representation** | Establishes whom or which organization the actor represents. | Use between identity and delegated authority when representation matters. | Working narrative. |
| **Role** | Describes the actor's function or permissions within a context. | Do not confuse roles with commercial user types or with authority itself. | Working narrative; commercial distinction is canonical internal. |
| **Authority** | Establishes whether the actor is institutionally permitted to perform the action. | One of the central Circularo narrative terms. Pair with identity rather than collapsing the two. | Working narrative. |
| **Institutional authority** | Authority recognized by the organization, government body, or operating institution. | Use when contrasting capability or intelligence with permission. | Working narrative. |
| **Delegated authority** | Authority granted to an actor to act within a defined mandate. | Essential in agentic and application-led execution. | Working narrative. |
| **Mandate** | The bounded scope within which a person, application, or agent may act. | Useful partner and architecture shorthand; define the actual policy and approval behind it. | Working narrative. |
| **Policy** | The conditions that determine whether and how an action may proceed. | Pair with authority, approval, and evidence. Avoid presenting policy as a vague compliance label. | Working narrative. |
| **Approval** | The required human or institutional decision before an action may proceed or complete. | Distinguish from signature when approval and execution are separate. | Working narrative. |
| **Assurance** | The degree and combination of confidence required in the actor, process, origin, execution, and evidence. | Treat as a business and risk decision before discussing technical mechanisms. | Working narrative. |
| **Assurance level** | A named or selected level of assurance appropriate to a trusted action. | Conceptual until jurisdiction, policy, provider, and product support are validated. | Working narrative; validation required. |
| **Workflow assurance** | Confidence that the required process and approvals were followed. | Use as one assurance dimension. | Working narrative. |
| **Personal assurance** | Confidence in the identity and authorization of the individual acting or signing. | Use as one assurance dimension. | Working narrative. |
| **Organizational assurance** | Confidence that an action officially originated from or was authorized by an organization. | Use as one assurance dimension. | Working narrative. |
| **Execution profile** | A reusable trust recipe mapping a business action and assurance requirement to the required trust events, providers, jurisdictional rules, and evidence. | Strong solution-design concept; do not imply preconfigured market profiles without validation. | Working narrative; product validation required. |
| **Trust boundary** | The explicit boundary within which identity, authority, policy, approval, execution, evidence, and audit remain governed. | Use across SaaS, sovereign, and agentic contexts. | Working narrative. |
| **Governed action** | An action bounded by identity, delegated authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit. | Use to describe the permitted output of people, systems, or agents. | Working narrative. |
| **Bounded action** | A concise way to describe an action constrained by mandate, policy, approval, and evidence requirements. | Use after those constraints are made explicit. | House recommendation. |

## 4. Evidence, records, and institutional memory

| Term | Working definition | Use | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidence** | The information retained to show what happened, who or what acted, under which controls, and with what result. | Present as an outcome of execution, not a technical afterthought. | Working narrative. |
| **Execution evidence** | Evidence tied directly to the action, its actors, authority, process, version, and outcome. | Useful when distinguishing evidence from a generic audit log. | House recommendation. |
| **Audit** | The ability to inspect the execution history, controls, events, and retained evidence. | Use with evidence; do not promise regulatory sufficiency without validation. | Working narrative. |
| **Auditability** | The quality of being traceable and reviewable after execution. | Prefer to unsupported claims such as “fully compliant.” | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted record** | A durable record that preserves the authoritative connection among actor, role, authority, version, decision, approval, trust events, timestamps, evidence, and audit history. | Central outcome term. Contrast with a stored file without dismissing document storage. | Working narrative. |
| **Verifiable record** | A record whose execution context and evidence can be checked later. | Useful audience-facing synonym when “trusted record” needs explanation. | Working narrative. |
| **Institutional evidence** | Durable evidence retained as part of the institution's accountable history. | Use when moving from transaction output to long-term value. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted archive** | An archive that preserves provenance, permissions, context, evidence, and verification—not only file location. | Use as a narrative contrast, not a formal product name unless approved. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted vault** | A visual or narrative label for protected trusted records and evidence. | Use sparingly; avoid presenting it as a distinct product without validation. | Working narrative; owner validation required. |
| **Institutional memory** | The accumulated, contextualized body of trusted records showing what the organization knew, decided, approved, and executed. | Strategic bridge from records to AI context. | Working narrative. |
| **Institutional knowledge** | Searchable, attributable knowledge derived from trusted records and their context. | Use as the informational output of institutional memory. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted context** | Context that is attributable, permission-controlled, historically grounded, governed, and auditable. | Preferred basis for the Circularo AI narrative. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted AI context** | Trusted context made available to AI under appropriate permission and governance. | Do not imply model accuracy, reasoning safety, or truthfulness. | Working narrative. |
| **Trusted intelligence** | AI-supported understanding or recommendation grounded in trusted context. | Use in the records-to-action progression; qualify capabilities. | Working narrative. |
| **Provenance** | The traceable origin and history of content, decisions, actors, and evidence. | Use when explaining why institutional memory is more than search. | Working narrative. |
| **Authoritative version** | The version that was reviewed, approved, executed, or preserved as the institutional reference. | Useful in fragmentation and evidence narratives. | Working narrative. |

## 12. Core chains and models

### Autonomy progression

> Paper → Digital → Automated → AI-Assisted → Agentic

Use to establish why the need for institutional control is increasing.

### Authority chain

> Actor → Authority → Policy → Approval → Execution → Evidence → Audit

Use when “Identity” has already been established. Otherwise begin with Identity.

### Full trust boundary

> Identity → Representation → Delegated Authority → Policy → Approval → Execution → Evidence → Audit

Use when the actor may represent another person or organization.

### Trusted lifecycle

> Create → Collaborate → Review → Approve → Sign or Seal → Evidence → Preserve → Retrieve → Analyze → Act

Adapt visible stages to the use case, but preserve the logic from creation through evidence and reuse.

### Evidence-to-action progression

> Trusted Records → Trusted Context → Trusted Intelligence → Governed Action

Use to connect records, institutional memory, AI, and execution.

### Action-to-outcome progression

> Trusted action → Trusted execution → Trusted and verifiable outcome → Trusted record

Use to distinguish the customer's intended action, the governed execution mechanism, the resulting business state, and the durable proof of that result.

### Circularo AI progression

> KNOW → ADVISE → ACT

Always state that ACT remains bounded by identity, delegated authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit.

### Shared-services adoption progression

> Common trust model → Initial shared capabilities → Priority entities and services → Proven operating model → Progressive expansion

Use to show that national or ecosystem architecture does not require immediate migration by every entity.

### Actor set

> People · Organizations · Applications · AI Agents

Use consistently when showing who or what can participate in the trust environment. Add citizens, employees, external parties, portals, or enterprise systems only when the audience requires the more specific channel or participant view.

### Platform capability lens

> Cloud · Identity · Collaboration · Sign & Seal · DMS / Trusted Vault · AI

This is a working six-capability portfolio lens from the source material. It is different from the execution chain and requires product-owner validation before it becomes a numbered external capability model.

### Access, execution, and trust lens

- **Access** — enable participation by people, organizations, applications, and external actors.
- **Execution** — enable workflows, documents, APIs, automation, and AI to perform business processes.
- **Trust** — apply the identity, authority, assurance, trust services, evidence, and records required for the action.

Use as a strategic explanatory lens, not as a commercial layer model.

### Trusted-execution flywheel

> Trusted collaboration → Trusted execution → Institutional evidence → Trusted records → Trusted AI context → Governed agentic action → New trusted execution and evidence

Use only when a cycle materially improves understanding. For shorter content, prefer the linear evidence-to-action progression.
