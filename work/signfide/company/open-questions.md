Basis: Josef’s concerns about REDA performance, avoiding an indefinite commitment, and preserving global brand options; the [company brief](README.md), [RACI](cooperation-responsibilities-and-raci.md), and [contribution schedule](reda-contribution-and-performance.md).

The founders need an arrangement that can succeed in the United States while preserving Circularo’s technology and future brand opportunities. The company ownership, REDA’s operating appointment, technology supply, brand license, and territory rights are separate decisions. A remedy in one agreement must not be assumed to resolve all the others.

The starting company proposal remains JP Investment LLC 60% and REDA’s team 40%, with Option A for technology operations. No trademark owner, equity remedy, exclusivity, exit right, or worldwide entitlement is settled by this registry. “Developing the brand together” in earlier drafts describes cooperation; it does not establish joint trademark or global brand ownership.

## How to use the registry

- **P0:** resolve before signing definitive company/cooperation/license documents or committing the affected assets or rights. **P1:** resolve before customer launch. **P2:** resolve before the relevant expansion or change.
- Listed leads are proposed discussion coordinators, not appointed legal decision-makers. Name the authorized approvers, action owner, and actual date when assigning an item.
- Record a decision, rationale, approvers, date, and implementing document/clause before marking an item resolved. A discussion preference or legal research note is not an agreed contractual right.
- Retain IDs when decisions change. Record a successor decision and reopen affected dependencies rather than silently replacing the history.
- Keep confidential customer/contact evidence in the owning business systems. This registry holds questions and aggregate evidence references only.

## SF-OQ-001 REDA underperformance

**Question:** What specifically happens if REDA supplies little or no effective commercial operation, or the US venture produces no reasonable results?

**Exposure:** Circularo could continue providing technology, support, favorable terms, and brand access while the commercial contribution supporting REDA’s interest does not materialize.

**Decisions required:**

- Agree named resources, capacity, accepted launch deliverables, qualification rules, and commercial targets using the [contribution schedule](reda-contribution-and-performance.md).
- Distinguish failure to deliver committed work from disappointing results despite agreed execution, and from delays caused by Circularo or other dependencies.
- Define review periods, material shortfall, notice, corrective action, evidence, and a decision process that cannot remain unresolved indefinitely.
- Decide the available commercial responses: replacement of personnel, replacement of REDA as commercial operator, loss of any conditional exclusivity, non-renewal, or termination of the relevant appointment/license.
- Decide separately whether and how an equity remedy is available; see SF-OQ-002. Ending an operating appointment should not be assumed to remove an ownership interest.

**Proposed discussion position:** Continued privileged commercial rights should depend on objectively agreed delivery and performance conditions, with a defined corrective process and an executable fallback. Neither indefinite patience nor automatic forfeiture is an agreed outcome.

**Closure evidence:** Completed performance schedule and agreed shortfall/remedy provisions mapped to the affected agreements, including treatment of existing customers.

## SF-OQ-002 REDA’s proposed 40 percent interest

**Question:** Is the 40% interest issued at the outset, earned over time or milestones, subject to an agreed repurchase mechanism, or structured another way?

Compare the alternatives with counsel, including treatment of delivered versus undelivered contribution, ordinary poor market outcomes versus material breach, departure of key people, valuation, payment timing, and tax/accounting implications. Do not assume a missed sales target permits confiscation or transfer of an interest.

**Proposed discussion position:** Agree the contribution and downside mechanics before implementing the ownership arrangements. Identify who may exercise any right, who must approve it, how disputes are resolved, and how the payment is funded. The proposed 60/40 split itself remains unchanged unless the founders agree otherwise.

**Closure evidence:** Company documents and any related contribution/transfer provisions reconciled with the performance schedule. No numeric valuation, vesting period, or repurchase price is proposed here.

## SF-OQ-003 Separate supplier exit from investor exit

**Question:** Which obligations could continue indefinitely, and what releases Circularo and JP Investment from each of them?

| Relationship | Exit question |
| --- | --- |
| Circularo as technology supplier | Contract term, renewal, breach, non-payment, termination, service transition, and any continuing commitments |
| Brand owner as licensor | Term, performance conditions, quality control, renewal, withdrawal, and debranding |
| REDA as commercial operator | Appointment duration, resources, replacement, handover, and surviving obligations |
| JP Investment as LLC member | Transfer, agreed exit/buyout rights, valuation, approvals, deadlock, and dissolution routes |
| SignFide as customer contracting entity | Ongoing subscriptions, notices, records, refunds, transition costs, and liabilities |

**Legal point to address:** Delaware’s default member-resignation rule can prevent withdrawal before dissolution unless the LLC agreement provides otherwise. A member exit must therefore be addressed expressly rather than inferred from a supplier termination clause. [Delaware section 18-603](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc06/index.html).

For voluntary dissolution, the statutory default uses more than two-thirds of profit interests unless the LLC agreement provides otherwise. JP Investment’s proposed 60% should not be treated as a guaranteed unilateral dissolution route. Court-ordered dissolution is a separate statutory process, not a pre-agreed commercial exit. [Delaware sections 18-801 and 18-802](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc08/index.html).

**Proposed discussion position:** Negotiate workable contractual and investment exit paths at the start, with funded valuation/payment mechanics and customer continuity. Specify whether termination of one relationship ends, changes, or leaves the others intact.

**Closure evidence:** One written exit map identifying each legal party, right, trigger, approval, cost, survivor obligation, and implementing clause. Circularo and JP Investment must be identified separately.

## SF-OQ-004 Global brand ownership

**Question:** Should Circularo, or a deliberately selected IP-owning affiliate, own the SignFide brand portfolio and license defined US use to SignFide LLC?

| Structure for discussion | Strategic effect and issue to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Circularo or selected IP entity owns the brand; US LLC is a licensee | Separates global brand control from the local venture. Requires a legitimate chain of title, proper trademark use/control, and a commercially workable US license. |
| SignFide LLC owns the brand | Concentrates brand rights and US operations in the joint company. Decide whether global expansion, sale, enforcement, and future licenses would require company approvals or economic participation. |
| Joint trademark ownership | Requires explicit rules for licensing, enforcement, approvals, transfers, costs, and dispute resolution. Do not confuse collaborative brand creation with a settled joint ownership arrangement. |

**Proposed discussion position:** If preserving independent global development is a priority, prefer examining ownership outside the US venture, with a defined US license. This is a proposed negotiating structure, not a conclusion that Circularo already owns the name or that it is available to register. Confirm existing rights and contributions before moving or assigning assets.

The trademark owner and filing entity must be correctly identified; USPTO warns that naming the wrong owner can require a new application. [USPTO ownership guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/common-problems).

**Closure evidence:** Selected legal owner, clearance strategy, existing-asset inventory, required assignments/permissions, filing instructions, registration/enforcement funding, and agreed license outline. Link to SF-OQ-010 for the non-trademark assets.

## SF-OQ-005 US license and exclusivity

**Question:** What exact rights should SignFide receive so it has a viable US business without controlling the brand’s future everywhere?

Set the territory, permitted products/services, channels and customer segments, brand standards, sublicense/transfer rights, duration, renewal, fees if any, approved enforcement process, and grounds for termination. Decide whether US rights are non-exclusive or whether exclusivity is conditional on performance. Reserve any existing Circularo business, channels, or accounts explicitly where intended; do not assume a SignFide brand license excludes all Circularo sales in the US.

The quality-control provisions need actual implementation, not only contractual wording: USPTO guidance links licensed trademark use and ownership to control over the nature and quality of the goods/services. [USPTO TMEP 1201.03](https://mpep.uspto.gov/RDMS/TMEP/print?href=TMEP-1200d1e7.html&version=current).

**Proposed discussion position:** A clear, bounded license with performance conditions and reasonable renewal/cure provisions. Protect REDA’s agreed US investment and customer commitments while preserving remedies for material nonperformance. Avoid both an unrestricted perpetual grant and an arbitrary withdrawal right that makes the US business unfinanceable.

**Closure evidence:** Reviewed US license term sheet and an agreed process for performance reviews, approval, cure, termination, and transition. No exclusivity or royalty is assumed here.

## SF-OQ-006 Global expansion and cross-border business

**Question:** If SignFide expands beyond the US, does REDA participate automatically, have an option to participate, or require a separate agreement?

**Proposed discussion position:** Reserve non-US development and licensing to the selected brand owner, subject to the final agreements and existing rights. Treat REDA’s proposed 40% as an interest in the US company, without an assumed interest in other ventures or the global brand portfolio. This boundary needs explicit agreement, not an inference from a company name or ownership percentage.

Define how to classify a customer’s territory: contracting entity, headquarters, bill-to location, end-user locations, online inbound enquiries, referrals, multinational groups, and global contracts. Decide lead routing, permitted servicing, channel conflict, and any referral compensation separately. Expansion might use regional entities or a global provider; no structure is selected here.

“Global brand ownership” is a portfolio strategy, not one worldwide trademark right. WIPO describes trademark protection as territorial and identifies national, regional, and international filing routes. [WIPO protection guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks/protection).

**Closure evidence:** Reserved-territory clause, cross-border account rules, expansion approval/economics, and a staged protection plan for intended markets.
