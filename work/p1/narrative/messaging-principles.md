# Circularo Messaging Principles

## 1. Lead with the buyer's trust problem

Start with the change in digital work and the resulting accountability problem. Do not open with a feature inventory or an abstract claim that Circularo is “AI-ready.”

Preferred:

> As autonomy increases, the burden of trust increases.

Avoid:

> Circularo is the all-in-one AI-powered eSignature solution.

## 2. Make authority distinct from identity

Identity establishes who or what the actor is. Authority establishes whom the actor represents and what the actor may do. Policy and approval determine what may execute in a specific context.

Do not imply that identity alone establishes permission to act.

## 3. Treat the signature as one trust event

The trust chain starts when consequential content is created and continues through review, approval, execution, evidence, and preservation.

Preferred:

> The signature is one important trust event within a governed lifecycle.

Avoid diminishing the importance of signatures; broaden the frame without dismissing the existing category.

## 4. Use one continuous chain

The preferred lifecycle is:

> Create → Collaborate → Review → Approve → Sign or Seal → Evidence → Preserve → Retrieve → Analyze → Act

Adapt the visible steps to the audience, but preserve the logic from creation to evidence and reuse.

## 5. Explain evidence as an outcome

Do not present evidence and audit as technical afterthoughts. Show that the process produces a trusted record connecting actor, authority, version, decision, execution, and proof.

## 6. Connect records to AI credibly

The AI story begins with trusted context, not autonomous action.

Use the progression:

> KNOW → ADVISE → ACT

Make clear that ACT remains bounded by identity, delegated authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit.

## 7. Define the AI boundary precisely

Preferred:

> Circularo governs and evidences what an agent is permitted to execute.

Avoid:

> Circularo makes AI trustworthy.

Do not claim control over model reasoning, accuracy, or safety unless a specific approved capability supports the statement.

## 8. Make the API strategic

Describe APIs as the channel through which the same trust rules and evidence model can serve people, portals, enterprise systems, applications, and AI agents.

Avoid treating the API as a late feature-list item.

## 9. Keep the architecture stable and the emphasis flexible

All four decks inherit the same core logic. They do not inherit the same slide count or feature emphasis.

- Company emphasizes category and strategic relevance.
- SaaS emphasizes fragmented work, unified experience, adoption, and managed delivery.
- Sovereign emphasizes control boundaries, integration, and operating responsibility.
- Shared Services emphasizes reuse, governance across entities, and national-scale adoption.

## 10. Keep terminology controlled

Use consistently:

- Trusted Execution Layer — Circularo's architectural role.
- Continuous evidence chain — the lifecycle connection.
- Trusted record — the durable outcome of a completed execution.
- Institutional memory — the accumulated, contextualized body of trusted records.
- Governed action — an action bounded by authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit.
- System of Authority — the strategic outcome, pending external terminology approval.

Avoid introducing competing labels such as “execution fabric,” “trust operating system,” or “autonomy control plane” unless the messaging owner deliberately changes the vocabulary.

## 11. Prefer plain claims to slogan density

Use no more than one major coined concept per slide. Explain its consequence in natural language.

Preferred:

> Disconnected handoffs separate the final record from the decisions that produced it.

Avoid:

> Unified sovereign intelligent trust orchestration for the autonomous future.

## 12. Use American English consistently

Use “organization,” “authorized,” and “analyze” throughout the four deck applications unless a target market requires localized spelling.

## 13. Separate current capability from direction

Use present-tense product claims only when they are supported by a current approved record. Use clearly qualified future language for strategic direction.

Examples:

- Current and approved: “Circularo supports …”
- Directional: “Circularo is designed to …” or “The platform can provide a foundation for …”

## 14. Close with a concrete buyer action

Each deck should end with a next step matched to its buying problem:

- Company: choose the relevant application and business priority.
- SaaS: identify the first trusted workflow.
- Sovereign: define the sovereignty boundary.
- Shared Services: create the shared trust architecture blueprint.
