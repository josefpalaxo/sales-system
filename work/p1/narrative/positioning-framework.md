# Circularo Positioning Framework

## Positioning stack

Use these terms as a hierarchy, not as interchangeable slogans.

| Level | Preferred language | Role in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market context | Digital trust infrastructure | Names the broad space in which Circularo operates. |
| Strategic category | Trusted execution infrastructure | Describes the emerging requirement created by automated and agentic work. |
| Platform | Circularo Unified Trust Platform | Names the connected platform proposition. Product-owner validation is required before external standardization. |
| Architectural role | Trusted Execution Layer | Explains where Circularo sits between actors, institutional controls, and consequential actions. |
| Strategic outcome | System of Authority | Explains the organizational result: authoritative answers about who acted, under what authority, what executed, and what evidence remains. |

## Positioning statement

> For organizations and governments that need consequential digital actions to remain authorized, governed, and provable, Circularo provides a trusted execution layer connecting identity, authority, policy, collaboration, approval, trust services, evidence, and records. Unlike isolated workflow, eSignature, or archive tools, Circularo maintains a continuous evidence chain across the full lifecycle and makes the same trust capabilities available to people, applications, and AI agents.

## Value hierarchy

### Functional value

- Bring collaboration, approval, signing or sealing, evidence, and records into one governed lifecycle.
- Connect existing identity, trust-service, and enterprise infrastructure.
- Expose trusted execution capabilities through APIs.

### Operational value

- Reduce fragmented handoffs and duplicated integration work.
- Retain process context and evidence across the lifecycle.
- Start with a specific workflow and expand progressively.

### Governance value

- Establish who or what acted, under which authority and policy.
- Apply the required approval and assurance to consequential actions.
- Preserve verifiable evidence of execution.

### Strategic value

- Turn trusted records into institutional memory.
- Provide governed context for AI.
- Enable agentic action without separating autonomy from accountability.

## Competitive contrast

The primary contrast is not “Circularo has more features.” It is the scope of accountability.

| Isolated approach | Circularo narrative |
| --- | --- |
| Digitize the signature. | Govern the complete execution. |
| Connect separate workflow, signature, and archive tools. | Maintain one continuous trust and evidence chain. |
| Store the final file. | Preserve the trusted record and its execution context. |
| Add AI to disconnected repositories and processes. | Give AI governed context and bounded actions. |
| Treat APIs as integrations to an application. | Make trust capabilities programmable across channels and actors. |

## Deployment propositions

### Circularo SaaS

Managed trusted execution for organizations that want a faster path to unified digital work without operating the platform infrastructure themselves.

### Circularo Sovereign

Customer-controlled trusted execution for organizations whose infrastructure, data, identity, keys, policy, evidence, AI, or operations must remain within a defined sovereignty boundary.

### Sovereign Trust Shared Services

Reusable trusted execution infrastructure established once and consumed across ministries, agencies, public services, applications, and approved AI agents.

## Language boundaries

- Do not position Circularo as a replacement for national identity, enterprise IAM, PKI, certification authorities, HSMs, timestamp authorities, or trust-service providers. Position it as the layer that connects those controls to business execution and evidence.
- Do not claim that Circularo governs AI reasoning. State that Circularo governs and evidences permitted execution.
- Do not use “System of Authority” as an established external category until the accountable owner validates it. Until then, present it as the strategic outcome of the platform.
- Do not collapse SaaS, Sovereign, and Shared Services into deployment variants. Each answers a different buying problem.
