# Recommendations

## 1. Approve the conceptual hierarchy before visual production

Confirm one owner-approved meaning for each term:

1. Digital trust infrastructure — market context.
2. Trusted execution infrastructure — strategic category.
3. Circularo Unified Trust Platform — platform proposition.
4. Trusted Execution Layer — Circularo's architectural role.
5. Continuous evidence chain — the lifecycle mechanism.
6. Trusted record and institutional memory — accumulated value.
7. System of Authority — strategic outcome.

This decision will prevent the visual decks from turning every strong phrase into a competing headline.

## 2. Approve the core narrative as modular, not mandatory in full

The shared narrative should be the source for all four decks, but each deck should use only the modules needed for its buyer question.

- Company uses almost the full strategic chain.
- SaaS compresses the autonomy and authority material and focuses on fragmented work, managed delivery, adoption, and evidence.
- Sovereign emphasizes the control boundary, integrations, operations, and sovereign AI.
- Shared Services emphasizes government-wide reuse, entity governance, and progressive adoption.

## 3. Validate “System of Authority” before making it the external category headline

The term is distinctive and valuable, but it carries a large category claim. Validate it with product, sales, legal, and selected customers or partners.

Recommended interim use:

> The strategic outcome is a System of Authority.

Avoid, until approved:

> Circularo is the world's System of Authority platform.

## 4. Create a governed product-capability map

Before final slides are produced, map every narrative element to current capability, configured capability, partner/integration capability, or product direction.

The map should cover:

- identity and delegated authority;
- policy and approvals;
- collaboration and versioning;
- signing, sealing, timestamps, and verification;
- evidence, audit, archive, and retrieval;
- APIs and automation;
- AI: Know, Advise, and Act;
- SaaS delivery;
- sovereign deployment and operations; and
- shared-services tenancy and governance.

This will prevent a strategic narrative from silently becoming an unsupported feature promise.

## 5. Build an approved proof pack before external use

Each deck needs proof matched to its buying problem:

| Deck | Proof needed |
| --- | --- |
| Company | Company scale, markets, deployment footprint, customer types, or approved strategic credibility. |
| SaaS | A workflow outcome, adoption result, integration result, or operational improvement. |
| Sovereign | A customer-controlled architecture, security boundary, deployment, integration, or operating result. |
| Shared Services | Multi-entity reuse, governance, onboarding, scale, interoperability, or cross-government outcome. |

Use attributable facts and governed customer references. Do not use generic logo walls as a substitute for evidence.

## 6. Keep the “Know → Advise → Act” model, with capability states visible internally

This is the clearest AI progression. Maintain an internal label for every item:

- available now;
- configurable or integration-dependent;
- in development;
- strategic direction.

Only the first two should appear as present-tense external capability claims unless the product owner approves different language.

## 7. Keep sovereignty concrete

Use a sovereignty-boundary model rather than a generic “on-premises” claim:

> Infrastructure · Data · Identity · Keys · Policy · Integrations · Evidence · AI · Operations · Jurisdiction

For every proposal, mark which party controls each dimension. This turns sovereignty into an architecture and operating-model discussion.

## 8. Define shared-services governance before emphasizing national scale

The Shared Services deck should not imply that shared infrastructure means shared unrestricted data.

The final solution narrative must define:

- platform ownership;
- entity and tenant boundaries;
- policy ownership;
- evidence and record ownership;
- identity and key control;
- onboarding authority;
- funding and service management; and
- operations and support.

## 9. Preserve the existing source drafts for traceability

Do not archive or delete the working ideas yet. The P1 package consolidates their content, but the original drafts retain useful provenance and alternative formulations.

Archive them only after the P1 narrative is approved and the repository owner decides how working-source history should be retained.

## 10. Use a content lock before slide design

Recommended approval sequence:

1. Messaging owner approves the core narrative and terminology.
2. Product owners validate capability wording.
3. Proof owners supply approved evidence.
4. Sales owners validate each buyer journey and next step.
5. Legal or compliance reviews externally sensitive claims.
6. Content is locked for visual production.

Visual design should begin only after items 1–3 are sufficiently complete to avoid redesigning the decks around corrected claims.
