# Sovereign Trust Shared Services Deck

## Deck definition

**Primary audience:** Digital-government authorities, ministries responsible for digital transformation, national digital-identity authorities, government-cloud operators, cybersecurity authorities, national technology operators, and sponsors of cross-government shared services.

**Purpose:** Establish trusted execution as reusable sovereign infrastructure that can be governed once and consumed across many government entities.

**Communication job:** By the end, the audience should understand why entity-by-entity trust stacks create systemic fragmentation, see the value of a shared sovereign execution layer, and be ready to define a national or ecosystem trust architecture blueprint.

**Primary next step:** Run a multi-stakeholder discovery and shared-services architecture workshop.

## Narrative arc

National infrastructure need → Entity-by-entity fragmentation → Shared trust model → Reusable services → Government-wide evidence → Agentic readiness → Governed adoption

## Slide-by-slide content

### Slide 1 — Sovereign Trust Shared Services

**Build trusted execution once. Govern it centrally. Make it reusable across government.**

A common sovereign trust layer for ministries, agencies, public services, applications, and approved AI agents.

### Slide 2 — Digital trust is becoming shared national infrastructure

Governments have established shared infrastructure for identity, networks, payments, cloud, and cybersecurity.

As public services become more automated and agentic, trusted execution becomes another reusable infrastructure layer.

### Slide 3 — Government transactions are becoming machine-executable

Paper → Digital → Automated → AI-Assisted → Agentic

Applications and AI agents will increasingly prepare, initiate, and execute public-sector processes.

As autonomy increases, government must strengthen authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit at the point of execution.

### Slide 4 — National identity answers who; trusted execution answers what may happen

**Who are you?**  
National identity

**Whom do you represent?**  
Organization and role

**What may you do?**  
Delegated authority

**What was approved and executed?**  
Policy, workflow, trust services, evidence, and audit

Identity is foundational. It is not the complete execution model.

### Slide 5 — When every entity builds its own stack, government inherits fragmentation

Each ministry or agency separately procures workflow, eSignature, trust-service integrations, verification, records, audit, automation, and AI.

The result is duplicated integration, inconsistent governance, incompatible evidence models, and uneven service experiences.

### Slide 6 — The alternative is trust as a shared service

**Build once → Govern centrally → Consume across government**

Shared infrastructure establishes common trust capabilities while allowing each entity to configure its own services, roles, policies, and adoption path.

### Slide 7 — A national Trusted Execution Layer connects identity to evidence

**Citizens · Employees · Organizations · Applications · AI Agents**

↓

**SOVEREIGN TRUSTED EXECUTION LAYER**

↓

**Identity · Authority · Policy · Workflow · Approval · Sign & Seal · Trust Services · Evidence · Records · Audit**

### Slide 8 — This is enabling infrastructure, not another government application

The platform provides reusable execution and trust capabilities.

Ministries, agencies, municipalities, authorities, and government-owned organizations use those capabilities to build and operate their own trusted services.

> One shared trust model. Many entity-owned services.

### Slide 9 — Reusable services reduce repeated integration

Shared capabilities can include:

**Workflow · Approval · eSigning · eSealing · Timestamps · Identity integration · Evidence · Trusted records · Verification · APIs · Governed AI execution**

Each entity consumes the capabilities it needs within the shared governance model.

### Slide 10 — Preserve existing national investments

The shared layer should connect to:

**National eID · Government IAM · PKI and CA · HSM · TSA · Trust-Service Providers · Government Cloud · Line-of-Business Systems**

Circularo's role is to connect this infrastructure to business execution and evidence—not to replace it unnecessarily.

### Slide 11 — One evidence model improves government-wide accountability

Create → Review → Approve → Execute → Evidence → Preserve → Verify

A shared execution layer can establish consistent evidence principles while retaining clear entity ownership, permissions, and records boundaries.

The result is stronger auditability without collapsing every entity into one application or one data domain.

### Slide 12 — Trusted records can become institutional memory at every level

**Entity records** preserve the context of local decisions and services.

**Shared evidence standards** make execution more consistent and verifiable across government.

**Permission-controlled knowledge** can support cross-government learning where policy and classification allow it.

### Slide 13 — Agentic government requires governed execution

AI can understand, recommend, generate, and plan. It does not acquire public authority by itself.

An approved agent may initiate a service, prepare an action, or invoke a permitted trust capability only within:

**Identity → Delegated Authority → Policy → Approval → Execution → Evidence → Audit**

### Slide 14 — APIs make the trust layer reusable across channels

The same trust capabilities can serve:

**Government portals · Mobile services · Ministry systems · Shared platforms · Approved AI agents**

through governed APIs with consistent policy and evidence.

### Slide 15 — Sovereignty and shared governance must be designed together

The operating model must define:

- platform ownership and funding;
- infrastructure, data, identity, and key control;
- entity and tenant governance;
- service onboarding and policy boundaries;
- operations, security, support, and continuity;
- evidence ownership and access; and
- interoperability and lifecycle management.

### Slide 16 — Start with a shared architecture and a small number of high-value services

The architecture can be national from the beginning without requiring every entity to migrate at once.

**Common trust model → Initial shared capabilities → Priority entities and services → Proven operating model → Progressive expansion**

### Slide 17 — Prove the model across more than one entity

An initial program should demonstrate:

- reuse of the same trust capabilities;
- clear entity ownership and governance;
- integration with existing national infrastructure;
- consistent evidence across services;
- measurable operational or citizen-service outcomes; and
- a repeatable onboarding model.

### Slide 18 — Define the shared trust architecture

**National trust discovery → Stakeholder alignment → Architecture blueprint → Initial services → Multi-entity pilot → Progressive expansion**

Begin with the common trust capabilities that will remove the most duplication and strengthen accountability across government.
