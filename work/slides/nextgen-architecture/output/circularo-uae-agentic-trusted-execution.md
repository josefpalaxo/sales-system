# Trusted Execution for the Agentic Era

## Presentation brief

- **Audience:** Digital Dubai Authority (DDA) and TDRA leadership.
- **Purpose:** Expand existing digital trust services toward a shared government service supporting agentic AI.
- **Format:** Seven executive slides.
- **Core message:** Circularo extends existing digital identity, signature and evidence infrastructure into a common trust layer through which people, government systems and AI agents can execute consequential actions under institutional control.
- **Readiness:** The unified trust API and agent execution are available, as confirmed by Circularo. The concrete example is digitally sealing a document with organisational identity on behalf of an employee.
- **Meeting outcome:** Align on the shared service scope and select one cross-entity journey.

## 1. One trust layer for government

**Key message:** Build a shared execution layer on top of the government’s existing digital trust infrastructure.

**Slide content:**

- **Participants:** People and organisations · Government systems · AI agents · Ecosystem partners.
- **Unified trust API:** A common interface connecting participants to trust services.
- **Trusted execution layer:** Actor and authority · Policy and approval · Execution and evidence.
- **Connected core services:** Cloud · Identity · Collaboration · Sign and seal · Trusted vault · AI.
- **Existing foundation:** UAE PASS, TDRA GovSign, Digital Dubai Digital Sign and existing government systems.

**Suggested visual:** A simple, editable architecture stack: participants → unified trust API → trusted execution layer → connected services and existing foundations.

**Presenter emphasis:** The proposed expansion reuses established infrastructure while defining a common institutional framework for agent-initiated actions.

## 2. Towards Agentic AI Trusted Execution

**Key message:** Extend established digital trust into agent-initiated government action.

**Slide content:**

1. **Digital signatures:** Identity, signing and timestamps.
2. **Trusted collaboration:** Approvals and multi-party workflows.
3. **Sovereign trust infrastructure:** Shared services, identity integration and evidence.
4. **Trusted execution:** Governed actions through connected trust services.
5. **Agentic trusted execution:** Agents invoke trust services on behalf of people and organisations within the agreed scope of authority.

**Available today:** An agent can digitally seal a document with organisational identity on behalf of an employee.

**Suggested visual:** Five ascending steps, with the final two emphasised as API-enabled capabilities.

**Presenter emphasis:** This is a progression in the use of trust infrastructure, not a claim that every government entity has deployed every stage.

## 3. Agent actions within institutional control

**Key message:** Government entities need consistent rules for who acts, what they may do and when human authorisation is required.

**Slide content:**

| Control | Executive question | Shared-service requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and context | Which employee and agent are involved? | Connect the action to its initiator and institutional role. |
| Authority and policy | What is the agent permitted to do? | Make delegated scope and applicable limits explicit. |
| Approval and oversight | When must a person authorise the action? | Define approval requirements for consequential actions. |

**Accountability after execution:** Retain what was requested, permitted and executed, with attributable evidence.

**Suggested visual:** Three control columns above a continuous evidence band.

**Presenter emphasis:** These are controls to agree and apply across the shared service. Individual implementations and approval triggers require confirmation for each journey.

## 4. One interface for end-to-end trust services

**Key message:** Government channels, enterprise systems and agents connect through a common API.

**Slide content:**

- **Identity and authority:** Actor context and the authority required to act.
- **Collaboration and approval:** Preparation, review and authorisation workflows.
- **Signatures and seals:** Personal signing and organisational sealing.
- **Time and verification:** Timestamps and validation of trusted digital outputs.
- **Evidence and audit:** Traceable actions, decisions and supporting evidence.
- **Records and retrieval:** Preservation and permissioned access to trusted records.

**Shared-service aim:** Reusable integration, consistent controls and a continuous evidence chain.

**Suggested visual:** Six service families under the unified trust API heading.

**Presenter emphasis:** This is the executive service catalogue for the proposed expansion. The detailed API and configuration scope should be agreed for the shared service.

## 5. Agent-initiated organisational sealing

**Key message:** A concrete capability is already available: an agent invokes Circularo’s API to seal a document on an employee’s behalf.

**Slide content:**

1. **Employee context:** The document and the employee on whose behalf the action is performed.
2. **Agent API request:** The agent requests the sealing action through the unified trust API.
3. **Organisation’s seal:** Circularo applies the organisation’s digital identity to seal the document.

**For shared government rollout:** Agree delegation, approval triggers, evidence and retention requirements for each entity.

**Suggested visual:** Employee context → Agent API request → Organisation’s seal.

**Presenter emphasis:** Organisational sealing is distinct from applying an employee’s personal signature. The example illustrates the capability; it does not specify a production API sequence or government deployment status.

## 6. Evidence becomes institutional memory

**Key message:** Preserve provenance and permissions so AI can work with trusted institutional context.

**Slide content:**

- **Trusted records:** Identity and provenance; decisions, signatures and seals; timestamps and evidence.
- **Trusted AI context:** Attributable information; permission-controlled access; historical decision context.
- **Governed action:** Retrieve and understand; advise with context; invoke permitted actions.

**Continuous cycle:** Each governed action can add new evidence to the institutional record.

**Suggested visual:** Trusted records → Trusted AI context → Governed action.

**Presenter emphasis:** Trusted context informs AI. Policy and authority continue to govern execution.

## 7. Expand toward a shared government trust service

**Key message:** Build on Digital Dubai Digital Sign, TDRA GovSign and UAE PASS to support shared government adoption.

**Slide content:**

1. **Agree the service model:** Service ownership and scope; entity authority and boundaries; common controls and evidence.
2. **Validate a shared journey:** Agent-initiated sealing; cross-entity operating process; onboarding and support.
3. **Expand the service catalogue:** Additional trust services; government channels; connected agent platforms.

**Proposed next step:** Align DDA and TDRA on the shared service scope and select one cross-entity journey.

**Suggested visual:** Three phases ending in a clear decision banner.

**Presenter emphasis:** The proposal concerns government service design, integration and adoption. Ownership and authority boundaries between Dubai and federal entities remain to be agreed.

## Preparation notes

- This outline accompanies `circularo-uae-agentic-trusted-execution.pptx` and is a working presentation document, not an approved product specification.
- Availability of the API and agent execution, including organisational sealing on an employee’s behalf, was confirmed by the user. Production-versus-pilot maturity and individual control implementations were not specified.
- Name the existing services without deployment counts. Digital Dubai’s public catalogue uses **Digital Sign**; the working brief also calls it **DigiSign**.
- Do not imply an agreed joint government programme, endorsement or existing government agent deployment.
