- Audience: digital-government authorities, ministries, national identity and cybersecurity authorities, government-cloud operators, national technology operators, and shared-services sponsors
- Use: internal shared-infrastructure narrative and multi-stakeholder architecture discovery

## Purpose

Establish trusted execution as reusable sovereign infrastructure that can be governed once and consumed across ministries, agencies, public services, applications, and approved AI agents.

The intended outcome is for stakeholders to see why entity-by-entity trust stacks create systemic fragmentation and be ready to define a shared sovereign trust architecture.

## Key points

- Trusted execution is becoming a reusable national or ecosystem infrastructure layer.
- Government transactions are becoming increasingly automated and machine-executable.
- National identity answers who the actor is; trusted execution governs what may happen and what evidence must remain.
- Entity-by-entity trust stacks duplicate integration and create inconsistent governance and evidence.
- A shared model can be built once, governed centrally, and consumed across government while preserving entity ownership.
- A sovereign Trusted Execution Layer connects citizens, employees, organizations, applications, and AI agents to policy, workflow, trust services, evidence, records, and audit.
- The platform is enabling infrastructure rather than another centralized government application.
- Reusable services reduce repeated integration and support progressive entity adoption.
- Existing national identity, government IAM, PKI, CA, HSM, TSA, cloud, and business systems should be preserved and connected where validated.
- A shared evidence model can improve accountability without eliminating entity-specific permissions or records boundaries.
- Trusted records support institutional memory and governed AI context at the appropriate entity or shared level.
- APIs make the same trust capabilities reusable across portals, mobile services, ministry systems, shared platforms, and approved agents.
- Sovereignty, funding, platform ownership, tenancy, evidence access, support, continuity, and lifecycle governance must be designed together.

## Story structure

National infrastructure need → entity-by-entity fragmentation → shared trust model → reusable services → government-wide evidence → agentic readiness → governed multi-entity adoption.

## Recommended use

- Align multiple public-sector stakeholders around a shared trust-infrastructure concept.
- Prepare a national or ecosystem architecture workshop.
- Identify initial shared capabilities and a small number of priority entities or services.
- Design a multi-entity pilot with explicit governance and evidence boundaries.

## Primary next step

Run multi-stakeholder discovery and produce a shared-services architecture blueprint, initial operating model, and pilot scope.

## Qualification

Approved shared-services or multi-entity deployment evidence remains required for external use. National scale, multi-tenancy, interoperability, onboarding, volume, availability, citizen outcomes, integrations, data sharing, and AI capabilities must not be inferred from this working narrative.
