- Use: internal sovereign-deployment narrative and architecture discovery

## Purpose

Show how an organization can pursue modern trusted-execution capabilities while retaining control of the infrastructure, data, identity, keys, policy, evidence, AI boundary, and operations that must remain sovereign.

## Key points

- Digital trust is becoming part of the organization's critical control environment.
- Greater automation and agentic action increase the sovereignty requirement.
- Sovereignty is a complete control boundary, not only a data-residency decision.
- The desired outcome combines modern platform capability with customer-controlled operation.
- Locally deployed but disconnected systems can still fragment accountability.
- Circularo Sovereign is positioned as a customer-controlled Trusted Execution Layer.
- The platform should connect to existing national identity, IAM, PKI, CA, HSM, TSA, trust-service, and enterprise infrastructure where validated.
- Identity establishes the actor while sovereign policy determines the permitted action.
- The evidence chain and trusted records remain within the agreed control boundary.
- Sovereign records can become sovereign institutional memory and governed AI context.
- Programmable trust should remain subject to the same authority, policy, approval, evidence, and audit model.
- Sovereignty is credible only when deployment, security, operations, upgrades, integration, keys, continuity, support, and lifecycle ownership are explicit.

## Story structure

Critical trust infrastructure → sovereignty gap → modern platform inside the control boundary → connected evidence and records → sovereign AI → integrations and operating model → pilot and boundary workshop.
