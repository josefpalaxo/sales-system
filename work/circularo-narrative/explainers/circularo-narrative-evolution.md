# Circularo narrative evolution

**Status:** Internal working explainer; strategic reasoning, not a product release history or approved capability statement.  
**Purpose:** Explain why the Circularo story expands while retaining digital signatures as a foundation.  
**Source:** [Original evolution note](<../sources/narrative-evolution/Circularo narrative evolution.md>), interpreted alongside [the orchestration explainer](circularo-trust-orchestration.md).  
**Working references:** [Dictionary](../dictionary/README.md) and [Strategic Narrative](../narratives/02-circularo-strategic-narrative.md).

## One continuous evolution

The story begins with a familiar question: who signed what, and what evidence supports that action?

That question remains important. The wider organizational requirement is to connect the signature to the content, authority and decisions around it. The organization needs to understand which version was reviewed, who approved it, what the participant could authorize and what evidence remains after completion.

Digital trust is the broad context for those questions. Trust Orchestration explains how the relevant controls and services are coordinated. Trusted Execution names the governed outcome. Agentic Trusted Execution extends that outcome model to participation by AI agents.

The working narrative sequence is:

**Digital Signatures → Trust Orchestration → Trusted Execution → Agentic Trusted Execution**

The original evolution note used **Digital Trust** as its second stage. The companion orchestration note made the mechanism more explicit. The current dictionary retains that distinction: digital trust describes the broader problem; Trust Orchestration describes the platform capability. Both notes contribute to the explanation.

## The scope expands around signing

A digital signature is an important trust event within a business process. Depending on the action, that process may also need identity checks, approval, an organizational seal, time evidence and preservation of the resulting record.

The platform story connects those events to their purpose. An approval records a decision within an authority model. Signing applies the required signature mechanism to the relevant content. Evidence helps establish what occurred and whether the intended result was achieved.

An individual trust event may be sufficient for a narrowly defined action. A broader business outcome may require several events and controls. This keeps the distinction between a trust event and a complete Trusted Execution clear.

## The unit of value becomes the governed outcome

The customer ultimately needs an agreement completed, an approval granted, an official document issued or another business action performed under its applicable requirements.

In this narrative, **Trusted Execution** is the governed completion of a trusted action under applicable authority, policy, assurance, regulatory and legal requirements, with evidence sufficient to verify the intended outcome.

“Trusted action” names the act to be governed; it does not certify that an unvalidated request or completed workflow has met that standard. Compliance and legal effect must be established for the relevant execution before they become claims about an actual result.

**Trust Orchestration is the platform capability. Trusted Execution is the outcome.**

## One platform vision supports different buying conversations

The source note correctly seeks a connected platform story across SaaS, Sovereign and Shared Services. Each conversation begins with a different customer need:

- **SaaS:** managed delivery that connects digital work and its evidence.
- **Sovereign:** the customer's required control boundary and the responsibilities needed to maintain it.
- **Shared Services:** reusable capabilities operated for participating entities with clear authority and record boundaries.

These are related buying narratives. They are not three equivalent hosting options. Deployment and operating responsibilities must be established separately, and the common vision does not prove feature parity. Exact commercial definitions remain in the governed records; the Sovereign label's deployment scope still needs owner confirmation.

## Agentic participation makes delegation explicit

An agent may be capable of preparing an agreement or initiating a workflow. The organization still determines whom it represents, what it may do, which decisions need approval and what evidence must remain.

**Intelligence does not create institutional authority.**

Circularo's strategic opportunity is to extend Trust Orchestration to those agent-participating processes. The proposed role concerns permitted action, execution and evidence. It does not require Circularo to become the agent or govern the model's internal reasoning.

That role applies to actions routed through the governed process. Connected systems need to return evidence of actual completion, and the wider design must address permissions and bypass paths. These are future-model requirements until their product support is confirmed.

## How to use the evolution story

Use the customer's current process to establish relevance. Explain the platform's orchestration role next, then introduce agentic participation when it matters to the audience.

Keep three kinds of statement distinct: what current evidence supports, how the platform is strategically positioned, and what the future agentic model is intended to achieve. The evolution provides continuity between them; it does not turn strategic direction into delivered functionality.
