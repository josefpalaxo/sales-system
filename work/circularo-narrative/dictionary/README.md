This working catalog is the common language reference for Circularo-related presentations, web copy, brochures, partner enablement, discovery, and internal narrative development.

## Fast-start vocabulary

Use this hierarchy when framing Circularo:

1. **Digital trust infrastructure** — broad market context.
2. **Circularo Trust Orchestration Platform** — working platform proposition connecting business processes, trust infrastructure, and evidence.
3. **Trust Orchestration Layer** — the platform mechanism coordinating actors, authority, policy, approvals, assurance, trust services, execution, and evidence.
4. **Trusted Execution** — the governed completion and trusted outcome of that orchestration.
5. **Agentic Trusted Execution** — the same outcome model extended to actions prepared, initiated, or performed by AI agents.
6. **System of Authority** — working institutional outcome, not yet an established external category.

**Circularo Unified Trust Platform** remains a working portfolio label. **Trust Orchestration Platform** is the more precise proposition because it explains what the platform does.

Use these buying narratives, distinguishing deployment from service operation:

- **Circularo SaaS** — managed Trust Orchestration producing Trusted Execution outcomes.
- **Circularo Sovereign** — customer-controlled Trust Orchestration producing Trusted Execution outcomes inside the required sovereignty boundary.
- **Sovereign Trust Shared Services** — centrally governed Trust Orchestration infrastructure reused across multiple entities.

SaaS describes managed delivery. Sovereign frames required customer control; supported deployments and responsibilities need confirmation. Shared services describes an operating and governance model across entities, not a third equivalent hosting option. Do not infer supported combinations or feature parity. Exact commercial deployment treatment remains in `edition-model:plan-edition-deployment`.

Use this core mechanism:

> Trusted action → Trust Orchestration → Trusted Execution → Trusted Record

Use this value progression:

> Trusted action → Trust Orchestration → Trusted Execution → Trusted record → Institutional memory → Trusted AI context → Governed action

Trusted Execution names the intended trusted, compliant and verifiable outcome standard, including legal effect where applicable. Each actual outcome claim requires scope and evidence. Institutional memory and AI context are strategic opportunities; retained provenance does not establish the truth of every statement or guarantee AI accuracy.

## Primary terminology decision

Use **trusted action** or **trusted business action** in new audience-facing content. Do not use **consequential action** as the default phrase.

- A **trusted action** is the specific business act to be prepared, approved, executed, and evidenced. The term states the trust requirement, not that the action has already passed its controls. Use “proposed action” or “business action” when describing an unvalidated request.
- **Trust Orchestration** is the platform capability that coordinates content, actors, identity, authority, policy, approvals, assurance, trust services, execution, evidence, audit, and records according to the context of the action.
- **Trusted Execution** is the governed completion of a trusted action under applicable authority, policy, assurance, regulatory and legal requirements, with evidence sufficient to verify the intended outcome.
- A **verifiable outcome** is a Trusted Execution that can be substantiated later through connected evidence and a trusted record.

For an action intended to create legal obligations, Trusted Execution includes applying and evidencing the conditions required for a legally binding outcome in the applicable jurisdiction. For other actions, the required outcome may be authorized, policy-compliant, auditable, and verifiable without being a legally binding transaction.

Use **trusted, compliant, and verifiable outcomes** as the intended customer-value standard. A claim that a particular execution meets it requires validation of its scope and evidence. Add **legally binding** when the use case is legally significant and its execution profile has been validated.

The established evolution framework remains **Digital Signatures → Trust Orchestration → Trusted Execution → Agentic Trusted Execution**. Digital trust is the broader context. This expresses strategic scope, not four completed releases.

This decision applies to new decks, web content, brochures, enablement, and revisions of working material. Historical sources remain unchanged.
