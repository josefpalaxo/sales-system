## Claims deliberately held for validation

The brief does not assert that Circularo already provides a complete agent-governance platform, a universal trust API, autonomous authority to sign for a person, or coverage of every agent action. It does not claim government-wide deployment, qualified trust-provider status, universal legal validity or independently proven savings.

The consolidation review held named government references and deployment definitions pending evidence. New public vendor material does not, by itself, settle those issues or grant customer-reference permission. The brief therefore uses government bodies as intended audiences and proposed participants, not customer proof.

Reviewed governance records include `company:circularo` (reviewed, internal; review due 27 February 2027) and `commercial-rule:api-access` (approved, internal). The company record does not supply an approved corporate description or category claim. “Trust Orchestration Platform” remains proposed positioning.

The open material conflict `conflict:add-on-workspace-scope` remains unresolved and was due for review on 15 September 2026. Nothing in the shared-service proposition settles commercial enforcement scope or promises workspace-scoped add-ons. No pricing, allowance or storage terms are reproduced. The other two inspected conflict records, concerning automated-transaction inclusion and transactional-document storage limits, are resolved; neither is needed to make this positioning case.

## Evidence required before expansion

The proposed pilot should produce a capability map, an agreed assignment of operational responsibilities, a process and authority definition, representative execution evidence, and a measurement report. These are proposed deliverables, not completed work.

| Question | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| Can an agent act only within its mandate? | Allowed and refused actions, expiry and revocation tests, and attempted bypass through connected systems. |
| Does approval cover what actually executes? | Exact content/version and material action parameters linked to the approval; changed content requires a fresh decision. |
| Is completion independently reconstructable? | Linked request, approval, trust event, downstream receipt and final state; failed and uncertain outcomes remain distinguishable. |
| Can the service be operated safely? | Duplicate-request handling, monitoring, suspension, recovery and human fallback demonstrated by named owners. |
| Are records and data under the required control? | Validated access separation, location and processing map, administrator and key responsibilities, retention and export/retrieval behavior. |
| Is the benefit real? | Baseline and pilot cycle times, handling effort, exception rates and the effort to reproduce the process elsewhere. |

Circularo product and architecture owners should validate availability; the government service owner should validate authority and operating responsibilities; the relevant legal and trust-services specialists should validate assurance requirements. External use requires the normal human review described in repository governance.
