#  From Digital Signatures to Trusted Execution

For the last decade, digital transformation has focused primarily on moving human processes online.

Documents became electronic.
Approvals became workflows.
Signatures became digital.
Identity moved online.
Cloud platforms replaced many internal systems.

That transformation is still continuing, but the operating model is changing again.

AI, automation and software agents are beginning to perform work that was previously performed exclusively by people.

The important question is therefore no longer simply:

> How do we enable a person to sign a document?

The broader question is:

> How do we know that a digital action was performed by the right person, organization, application or AI agent, under the right authority, following the right process, with evidence that can be trusted afterwards?

This is the transition from **digital signatures to trusted execution**.

---

# 1. Access Is Becoming Less Valuable. Trusted Execution Is Becoming More Valuable.

Traditional enterprise software economics were built around users.

More employees meant:

- more accounts
- more seats
- more licenses
- more software revenue

AI and automation fundamentally change this relationship.

A process that previously involved ten people may eventually involve:

- two employees
- one application
- several automated workflows
- multiple AI agents

The number of human users can decrease while the amount of work performed by the platform increases dramatically.

This means that **access alone becomes a weaker measure of value**.

What remains valuable is the execution of meaningful business actions and the trust surrounding those actions.

Identity.
Authority.
Approval.
Integrity.
Evidence.
Compliance.

Circularo therefore sees the future market increasingly organized around **trusted digital execution rather than software access alone**.

---

# 2. A Trusted Execution Is a Business Outcome

Customers do not want to buy cryptographic operations.

They want to accomplish business actions.

Examples include:

- approving an internal decision
- executing an employment agreement
- issuing an official government document
- signing a commercial contract
- validating a customer's identity
- executing an organizational seal
- approving a regulated transaction
- authorizing an AI agent to perform an action

Circularo describes these meaningful business actions as **Trusted Executions**.

A Trusted Execution represents what the customer is trying to achieve.

# 3. Every Trusted Execution Can Contain Multiple Trust Events

Behind a single business action, many individual trust services may be required.

For example:

### Employment Agreement

Identity verification

→ Organizational seal

Each of these individual operations is a **Trust Event**.

This distinction is important:

**Trusted Execution = customer value**

**Trust Events = underlying trust operations**

One Trusted Execution may contain:

- no external trust events
- one trust event
- multiple trust events

# 7. The Agentic AI Era Makes This More Important

Traditional applications primarily assisted people.

AI agents increasingly act.

They may:

- prepare documents
- evaluate information
- initiate workflows
- request approvals
- interact with external organizations
- execute API transactions
- generate official records
- trigger payments or contractual processes

This creates a new trust problem.

An organization needs to know:

**Which agent acted?**

**On whose behalf?**

**What authority did it have?**

**What information did it rely on?**

**Which policies governed the action?**

**What trust services were applied?**

**What exactly happened?**

**Can the execution be independently proven afterwards?**

AI therefore increases the importance of identity, authority and evidence rather than reducing it.

The more autonomous digital work becomes, the more important the **trusted execution infrastructure underneath it** becomes.
